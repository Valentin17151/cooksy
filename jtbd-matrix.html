What to build first, and what not to build at all

Twelve jobs against four personas. Every score carries its evidence; every cell the research cannot speak to stays visibly empty rather than being averaged into a number.

12 jobs × 4 personas = 48 cells
17 of them are unknown — and the pattern in which 17 is the finding

Build these three

scored 3 by the primary persona · not already closed by the market

Seven jobs score 3 for P-1. Four drop out immediately because the category already does them well — J-0, J-1, J-4 and J-6 are covered heavily, universally, thoroughly, and by three products respectively. Building there is building a worse Paprika. Three survive.

J-2

Using what I already have

Pain #1 in the entire corpus, and in-category coverage is genuinely poor. Paprika searches seven fields and still scores 3/5 on zero-recall find. The products that do this well — SuperCook, Eat Your Books — are outside the category and not competing here.

P-1 = 3 · P-3 = 3 · spec scored 23/40, below Tandoor

J-3

Amounts for the people actually eating

The market covers half of this and skips the half that matters. Everyone converts units; nobody constrains them. Crouton sells metric/imperial conversion as a feature — deliberately preserving the ambiguity — and Tandoor makes conversions user-editable.

P-1 = 3 · three-unit rule unique across all 15, validated by none

E-2

Careful about food without being technical

The only unoccupied position a primary persona scores 3 on. The optimiser's real choice today is a good model behind Docker, or a good app that fudges the maths. Not a feature and cannot be scheduled as one — it is the standard the other two are held to.

P-1 = 3 · 1 of 15 products is ingredient-first, and it is the least approachable

The fourth candidate, and why it is not in the three
H-1 — seeing what a total is made of would otherwise top this list: auditability is “an unoccupied position in the entire category, and the closest thing Cooksy has to a defensible wedge.” It is held back because it is a hypothesis, not an established job. The market gap is documented; the human desire behind it is graded “logic, not data”, and G-3 states it is not testable by desk research. Build it — but do not let it into a list labelled evidenced until someone has been watched either verifying a number or declining to.

Features that close no job

read straight off the function column
§5.6

Calorie reporting

Not one of the twelve jobs needs it — and the persona work points the other way. P-1 is explicitly not a diet persona, and health-goal seekers are recorded as an anti-persona. The dependent pieces fall with it: the finished-dish weight field exists mainly to make kcal/100 g honest.

Locked in §4 — a flag for a decision-maker, not a cut

§5.2.2

Sub-recipe links

Their only retrieval value is placing a recipe in the Used in tier — which J-2's function already reaches through method text and ingredient matching. The brief concedes the position: they “cost nothing to defer because nothing depends on them.”

A feature nothing depends on, serving a job nothing requires

§5.2.1

Ingredient groups

Serve no job directly. The justification that survives is narrow but real — parse fidelity, since a heading in the source is information the cook loses if dropped, plus one field on a search result row.

Weak case rather than a damning one

Two readings of the calorie finding, and the research cannot choose between them. Either a genuine job is missing because nobody went looking for “I want to know what I'm actually eating”, or the feature is unanchored. Both are live.

This is also the persona carrying a live “maybe there is no pain here” verdict: “a household with a working shared Keep list has no felt problem for Cooksy to solve.”

A secondary persona that is simultaneously the least understood and the one under a standing doubt is the cheapest place a single conversation would change the picture — and worth more than another pass over the same fifteen products.
