Four people, and what we don't know about them

Personas assembled from a corpus that contains no interviews, no teardowns and no user reviews. Every one of them is a hypothesis with a test attached.

Basis · 5 research docs, 15 products, 20 screens Primary · P-1 Systematic Optimiser Source of truth · research/personas.md

Method caveat — load-bearing

The corpus states of itself: “no hands-on teardown, no user interviews.” These personas are reconstructed from vendor-declared audiences and from what fifteen products chose to build or omit. Each persona is a falsifiable hypothesis about a user, not a measured segment.

The research also contains no real user reviews and no forum material. Every Voice below is therefore marked [?] and substituted with the closest line that actually exists in the corpus, labelled for what it is. None of them is a user speaking, and none should ever be quoted as one.

Why P-1 is primary — three reasons, all sourced

  1. The product loop is theirs. Four of §9's five success criteria — a real shop, trusted scaling, one pass through the store, nothing bought twice — are P-1's jobs almost verbatim. Every wedge the research found attacks a P-1 pain specifically: auditability as an unoccupied position (MP-3), the unique three-unit rule (D-3), the ingredient-first centre (D-2).
  2. The category default already targets them, which is the strongest external evidence any of these personas has: Paprika's audience is “multi-device home cooks who shop systematically.” vendor copy comparison.md · tier 1 — screen: t1-paprika.png
  3. Each of the other three hits a structural block in MVP. P-2 waits on household sharing, deliberately build step 8 of 8. P-3's entry path — the share sheet — is deferred post-MVP. P-4 collides with day-one cloud accounts and deferred export. Only P-1 is fully served by MVP as scoped.
P-1 Primary

The Systematic Optimiser

Already knows what to cook. Wants the logistics to disappear.

Context

Cooks regularly and already knows what they want to cook — needs a home recipe book that removes the overhead: scaling, merging, one list, no re-typing. The category describes this person only behaviourally: “multi-device home cooks who shop systematically. No demographic claimed; the UI selects for tolerance.” vendor copy

comparison.md · tier 1 — screen: t1-paprika.png

  • Where their recipes live today: [?] — hypothesis: a notes app plus browser bookmarks, per the research's own flagged assumption that Apple Notes is “plausibly where more recipes live than in every dedicated app combined.” assertion
  • Demographics — age, country, income: [?] Nothing in the corpus.
  • Not a diet persona. They want to know what a dish is, not track goals. “Health-goal seekers” are Samsung Food's declared audience and explicitly out of Cooksy's scope. vendor copy

Jobs

  • Get a recipe into the system faster than retyping it — the bar is set by pasting into a note.
  • Rescale portions without doing arithmetic. Crouton's inline scaling is the category's aspirational bar. screen t3-crouton.png
  • Walk the store once, off one merged, aisle-ordered list.
  • Query their own library by what's in the fridge — the “what can I make with the aubergine” job, pain #1 in the corpus.
  • Verify any merged number before trusting it.
  • Confirm the data can leave before typing sixty recipes in.

Pains, ranked

  1. Retrieval from their own hoard. “Storage is solved everywhere; usable storage nowhere.” correlation
  2. Numbers nobody shows the parts of — so they re-check merged quantities by hand. assertion The corpus grades this itself: “logic, not data.”
  3. Quantity chaos. A cup of flour and a cup of sugar differ by ~80 g, and all fifteen products preserve the ambiguity. correlation
  4. The notes-app residue — retyping, portion maths in the head, three laps of the supermarket. assertion
  5. The forced trade: “a good model behind Docker, or a good app that fudges the maths.” correlation

P-2 Secondary

The Household Logistics Lead

Runs the food logistics for two to four people. Needs one shared source of truth.

Context

Runs the plan, the shop and the split for a shared household. The market's words for them: “organised households, busy families… if you love meal planning and staying organized”; “households coordinating a shop”, and later “households mid-shop, in two different aisles.” vendor copy screen

They need the app as one shared source of truth, replacing a shared Keep list that has “no quantities, no ingredients, no semantics.”

  • Household size 2–4 is the brief's assumption, not research data [?]
  • The household contains a second member type who never authors anything and only ticks items off. assertion

comparison.md · tiers 1–3 — screens: t1-plan-to-eat.png · t2-anylist.png

Jobs

  • Keep one shared plan and one live list the whole household sees.
  • Split a shop in real time — two people, two aisles, basement signal.
  • Get portions × people right when different people eat different meals. [?] Hypothesis: the member-assignment mechanic is the brief's design, with no researched demand behind it.
  • Hand the list to the household member who won't install anything — Keep and Reminders are named as the export target.

Pains, ranked

  1. The honest one first: the pain may not be felt. “A household with a working shared Keep list has no felt problem for Cooksy to solve.” assertion
  2. The category default offers them nothing. Paprika has no household sharing at all, and its absence is now a defect rather than the norm. vendor copy correlation
  3. A half-finished shop gets destroyed when next week is planned — Paprika's one-list-at-a-time, called “a known complaint” whose complainant is unsourced.
  4. Lost or laggy checkoffs mid-shop. The bar is AnyList, which “has simply never lost a checkoff.” assertion

Why secondary

P-3 Secondary — risk

The Reel Hoarder

Saving is solved. Everything after saving is broken.

Context

Finds recipes where recipes are found in 2026 — Instagram and TikTok — and saves them in one tap: 114M reels under #recipe alone. screen The overflow lands in screenshots and notes, where “most ‘I'll organise it later' recipes die.” assertion

  • Age “under ~45” is the research's own unsourced estimate [?] — the only age-shaped datum in the entire corpus.

comparison.md · tier 2 — screen: t2-instagram-recipe-tag.png

Jobs

  • Capture in one gesture without leaving the video or page — the share-sheet job, which MVP explicitly defers.
  • Actually find a saved recipe weeks later.
  • Turn a saved blob into something cookable — portions, a list.
  • Own the save. Platform content can vanish, exports nothing, and saves are login-gated. correlation

Pains, ranked

  1. “Capture is effortless; retrieval is hopeless.” correlation
  2. A save is a bookmark on content you don't own — no search, no export, no quantities. correlation
  3. Video carries no pasteable text — and video import is rejected for MVP, so Cooksy's answer to their #1 source is “screenshot and retype”.
  4. Forms kill them fastest: “field-hunting is where libraries die at eleven recipes.” assertion

comparison.md · tier 2

Why secondary — and why “risk”

MVP is weakest exactly at their front door: share-sheet deferred, video rejected, and “the paste box carries the whole load.” Whether a hoarder ever converts into a library-builder is Condition X — the named live risk, with defined metrics and no data.

Chasing them first would also turn Cooksy into a capture product — the position the entire market already occupies.

P-4 Secondary — edge

The Data Sovereign

Wants Tandoor's model honesty without the sysadmin tax.

Context

Technical, with a large collection: “cooking enthusiasts with huge collections” — self-hosters. vendor copy screen Today they either run Tandoor behind Docker or refuse the category entirely, because the only ingredient-first product is “comfortably the least approachable of the fifteen.” correlation

comparison.md · tier 1 — competitive-landscape.md · pain 7 — screen: t1-tandoor.png

Jobs

  • Own and export everything, at GDPR grade. The Tandoor bar: open source, self-host, no tracking, 90-day export.
  • Get ingredient-level correctness — a real food database, editable conversions.
  • Pay once, or not at all. correlation

Pains, ranked

  1. The correct model ships inside the worst interface, and the setup cost keeps it technical. correlation
  2. Cloud competitors won't state offline, export or privacy anywhere on their sites — Fond, Plan to Eat and AnyList are all silent. correlation

Anti-personas — deliberately not served

The boundary is as load-bearing as the personas.

  • The health-goal tracker — Samsung Food's declared audience segment. Macros, goals and diet tracking are rejected in the brief. vendor copy
  • The discovery-seeker — Cookpad and Pinterest's audience: browsing for inspiration, social proof as ranking. The feed is rejected in the brief. vendor copy screen t2-cookpad.png

What would make these personas real

In priority order, matching the corpus's own next steps.

  1. The parser bake-off (G-1) tests P-1's and P-3's tolerance for mandatory review — the single riskiest assumption shared by the primary and the risk persona.
  2. The two week-one metrics (G-2: library size, median times-cooked) decide between P-1 and P-3 as the real centre of the product. Condition X made measurable.
  3. A month of real pantry and cooking use (G-7, G-8) tests P-1's ranked pains against lived behaviour.
  4. Any five user interviews would outweigh everything on this page. The corpus contains none.
