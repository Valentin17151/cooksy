Objects before screens

Twelve objects, nine screens, four doors and four flows drawn to their dead ends — every one of them derived from a job somebody has, and none of it from a competitor's menu.

12 objects · 9 screens · 4 doors 1 tap of navigation to the main job Coverage · 8/12 jobs · 8/9 screens Main job · J-0
Read this before using any of it

The jobs behind this architecture rest on a corpus with no interviews, no teardowns and no user reviews. Every job is inferred, so every object and every screen resting on one is inferred too.

And this page supersedes parts of the brief. Seven things changed on 5 August that CLAUDE.md still describes in full: the pantry, the household, URL import, the seven-day planner grid and full library export are gone; parsing is now done by a model; ingredient substitution was added. Where the brief and this page disagree, this page is current.

The sitemap

Nine screens · five intentions

Objects were derived from the jobs, screens from the objects. The five lines at the top level are intentions, not tabs — which of them become navigation is a separate argument, decided on frequency of use further down. Every screen carries the job it closes; one carries none and says so.

Nine screens, and nothing left over. Every candidate either became a screen or found a home inside one — which is the rule this pass kept arriving at: a thing that is the same object in a different state belongs on the same screen. A past grocery list is a grocery list. A past cook list is a cook list. A recipe somebody sent you is a recipe.

Four doors

Frequency of use, not feature count
Library J-2 · J-6 · J-1 Two of the three daily things happen here, and capture is a box on this screen rather than a place of its own.
Cook list J-0 · J-3 The decision half of the main job — and where changing the count changes what you buy.
Grocery list J-4 · J-0 · S-2 The payoff half, and the only screen used in a shop — trolley in one hand, bad light, no signal.
Account no job · architecture only Sign-in, who you are, this device's sync state, and the door to My ingredients. Not part of the loop, and it should not pretend to be.

keep  →  decide  →  buy   ·   the first three are the product's one sentence, left to right

To the job's surface 1 The list, in the shop, ready to tick.
To the decision point 1 The cook list, to say what we're eating.
Whole loop, cold, one recipe 5 Of which one is navigation — the other four are the job.

Navigation depth is 1, and nothing in the product sits deeper than two. The other four taps are the job, not the route to it: asking for the portion count is the single decision that makes every downstream number correct, and removing it would mean guessing.

Four flows, drawn to the dead ends

Main job + three related
a branch labelled yes a branch labelled no everything else — two right answers, not a right and a wrong one dashed node = a dead end

Green does not mean good. The colour marks which answer was given, not whether the outcome is desirable — "any flagged rows? — yes" is green and is bad news.

On loading states: Cooksy reads from the device, so most of it has none, and inventing them would misrepresent the product. Two are honest — the first sync onto a new device, and the model formatting a pasted recipe.

J-1Keeping what I find

Whensomething I want to cook goes past me, I wantto keep it in the seconds before it is gone, in a form that still makes sense three weeks later.

flowchart TD

    editor --> title("Name it")
    title --> qPhoto{"Add photos?"}

The five blanks, every one reviewed and accepted

Empty column

Account — a screen with no job

Architecturally necessary: an identity has to exist for sync and has to be reachable. A screen can be required by the architecture and required by no job at the same time — a real category the matrix cannot express.

Accepted · not a candidate for removal
Empty rows · J-5 · S-1

Both went with the household

Closed by objects that no longer exist. The rows are the pause, showing up where they should — and adding a screen would reverse a deliberate decision.

Accepted · household is paused
Empty row · E-3

Not screen-shaped at all

Closed by things not being built: no monetisation, no feed, no AI upsell, no drift to a content platform. A promise kept by refusal is still kept, and no screen was going to hold it.

Accepted · the row stays empty on purpose
Empty row · E-1

Bulk export is not being built

Not deferred — declined. A grocery list leaves as text and one recipe leaves as a link; the collection as a whole does not leave. The door is narrow rather than shut.

Accepted · scores 3 for P-4, 2 for P-1

The blanks stay in the matrix rather than being tidied away. A coverage table that shows only what is covered is a marketing document. The value of this one is that four jobs and one screen are visibly unaccounted for, on purpose, with a reason attached to each.

Every screen's states

Empty · loading · error

Every screen has empty, loading and error was true and useless while only four screens had theirs written down — they were specified where a flow happened to pass and nowhere else.

Two rules govern the whole table. An empty state names the one thing to do next and offers the control for it — an empty screen that only apologises is a dead end with better manners. And an error never blocks what is already on the device: the network failing is not a reason to stop reading a recipe.
